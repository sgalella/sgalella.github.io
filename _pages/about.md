To our senses, the world is never exactly the same twice. Light, viewpoint, distance and context change constantly, yet we recognize a cup as a cup, a face as the same face, a situation as one we have seen before. I'm interested in how brains find this **structure**: how they learn which differences matter and which can be ignored, turning a high-dimensional stream of sensory input into a small set of things they can reason about and act on. I see this ability, **abstraction**, as a hallmark of intelligence: treating different things as equivalent, extracting invariants such as objects, and generalizing to situations never encountered before.

I'm a PhD candidate in [Matthias Kaschube](https://www.fias.science/en/life-and-neurosciences/research-groups/matthias-kaschube/)'s group at the <span class="place">Frankfurt Institute for Advanced Studies (FIAS)</span> and <span class="place">Goethe University Frankfurt</span>, where I study how abstraction is reflected in the representations of **brains and artificial neural networks**, using tools such as dimensionality, representational geometry and topology. In recent work ([NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/116784)), I found that while the dimensionality of representations increases along the human visual hierarchy, deep vision models collapse it in their later layers, limiting their semantic generalization. This suggests that abstraction is not simply compression, but finding the right equivalences while keeping a rich space to read them out from. I also built [MAPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20549), a synthetic 3D dataset for probing vision models under controlled conditions.

Before my PhD, I spent two years as a deep learning engineer at <a class="place" href="https://www.neuroelectrics.com/">Neuroelectrics</a>, building MRI segmentation models for brain stimulation modelling, and worked as a research assistant on multitask neural networks at the <span class="place">Universitat Politècnica de València</span>. I hold a BSc in Biomedical Engineering and an MSc in Intelligent Interactive Systems from <span class="place">Universitat Pompeu Fabra</span>.
