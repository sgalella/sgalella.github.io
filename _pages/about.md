I'm a PhD student in [Matthias Kaschube](https://www.fias.science/en/life-and-neurosciences/research-groups/matthias-kaschube/)'s group at the <span class="place">Frankfurt Institute for Advanced Studies (FIAS)</span> and <span class="place">Goethe University Frankfurt</span>, where, as part of the <a class="place" href="https://neuroai-arena.github.io/team/">ARENA</a> project, I study abstract representations in **brains and artificial neural networks**.

What is abstraction? To our senses, the world is never exactly the same twice. Light, viewpoint, distance and context change constantly, yet we recognize a cup as a cup, a face as the same face, a situation as one we have seen before. I'm interested in how brains find this **structure**: how they learn which differences matter and which can be ignored, turning a high-dimensional stream of sensory input into a small set of things they can reason about and act on. I see this ability, **abstraction**, as a hallmark of intelligence: treating different things as equivalent, extracting invariants such as objects, and generalizing to situations never encountered before.

To study abstraction, I use tools such as dimensionality, representational geometry and topology on brain responses evoked by natural images and on neural networks. Looking ahead, I'm excited about using **virtual environments** to study abstraction in brains and encoding models: rendered 3D scenes, where factors such as object identity, pose, lighting and viewpoint are known and can be varied one at a time, let us treat representations as smooth functions of these factors and analyze them with differential and information geometry.

Before my PhD, I spent two years as a deep learning engineer at <a class="place" href="https://www.neuroelectrics.com/">Neuroelectrics</a>, building MRI segmentation models for brain stimulation modeling, and previously worked as a research assistant in several labs. I hold a BSc in Biomedical Engineering and an MSc in Intelligent Interactive Systems from <span class="place">Universitat Pompeu Fabra</span>.
